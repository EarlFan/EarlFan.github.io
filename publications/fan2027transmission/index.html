Transmission of a detonation wave across a hot inert gas layer

E. Fan, Yazhong Jiang, Xuxu Sun, Chih-Yung Wen, Lisong Shi

Combustion and Flame, 2027, 296, 115466

Download PDF DOI

Plain-language summary

We used computer simulations to study when a detonation wave can cross a layer of hot argon that does not react. In the simulations, the thickest layer the wave could cross first grew as the argon warmed, then shrank at higher temperatures.

Keywords · Detonation transmission, Hot inert layer, Detonation re-initiation, Spontaneous wave

Abstract

Parasitic combustion in rotating detonation engines produces hot gas layers that can disrupt detonation propagation. We use a pure-argon layer as a canonical idealization to isolate gas-dynamic effects. High-resolution one-dimensional (1D) and two-dimensional (2D) simulations examine a stable stoichiometric H₂/O₂/Ar detonation crossing this layer. The critical transmission thickness δc varies non-monotonically with inert-layer temperature Tᵢ. At the baseline Taylor-expansion pressure gradient Gₚ = 1.1 bar/cm, the success–failure bracket increases from [2.2, 4.4] cellular widths at 300 K to [6.6, 8.8] at 950 and 1700 K. It decreases to [4.4, 6.6] at 3000 K. Wave matching at the layer exit explains the associated non-monotonic change in downstream shock temperature. In 1D, the results support re-initiation through mutual amplification of pressure waves and chemical heat release. Broadening the trailing Taylor expansion reduces subsequent shock attenuation when the initial ZND structures are nearly identical. At 950 K, reducing Gₚ to 0.66 bar/cm shifts the critical bracket from [3, 4] to [4, 5] cm. In 2D, transverse waves compress the mixture and promote hot-spot formation. Reactions at these hot spots generate blast waves that further compress the mixture and interact to promote re-initiation. With matched baseline Taylor-expansion conditions, 1D and 2D simulations yield the same critical-thickness brackets at every sampled temperature. This agreement is limited to the 1 cm thickness resolution.
